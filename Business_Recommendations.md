# Olist Marketplace — Business Recommendations

## 1\. Executive Recommendation

The analysis shows a marketplace with strong commercial growth but a clear gap between **transaction growth and customer retention**. Delivered-order revenue reached **R$5.96M in 2017** and **R$7.22M in January–August 2018**, already exceeding the full 2017 revenue period. At the same time, only **3.00% of customers were repeat customers**.

The strongest strategic opportunity is therefore not simply to generate more first-time orders, but to **convert more first-time buyers into repeat customers while protecting the operational quality that supports retention**.

The recommended strategy is built around six priorities:

1. Increase repeat purchases and customer lifetime value.
2. Protect and develop high-value customers.
3. Use payment flexibility to support higher-value transactions.
4. Improve delivery reliability in the weakest regions and periods.
5. Strengthen and diversify the seller ecosystem.
6. Optimize the product/category mix using demand, value and satisfaction together.

\---

# 2\. Priority 1 — Increase Repeat Purchases

### Why this matters

Only **2,801 customers, or 3.00% of the customer base, were repeat customers**.

This is the most important commercial opportunity in the analysis because repeat customers generated an average of **R$260.05**, compared with **R$137.96** for one-time customers.

That means the average repeat customer generated approximately **88.5% more revenue** than a one-time customer.

### Recommended actions

#### 2.1 Build a first-to-second-purchase program

The most important retention objective should be moving customers from their first order to their second order.

Recommended actions:

* Send personalized follow-up campaigns after the first purchase.
* Recommend complementary products based on the first order.
* Provide limited-time incentives for a second purchase.
* Test free-shipping thresholds for returning customers.
* Trigger reminders based on expected product repurchase cycles.

### 2.2 Segment customers by value

Do not treat every first-time customer identically.

Create segments such as:

* High-value customers
* Medium-value customers
* Low-value customers
* First-time customers
* Repeat customers
* Recently inactive customers

High-value customers should receive more personalized retention efforts.

### 2.3 Analyze what creates repeat behavior

The next analysis should compare repeat and one-time customers by:

* product category,
* order value,
* payment method,
* delivery performance,
* seller,
* review score,
* geographic region,
* time between purchases.

This will identify the strongest predictors of a second purchase.

### KPIs

Track:

* Repeat customer rate
* Second-purchase conversion rate
* Average revenue per customer
* Customer lifetime value
* Average number of orders per customer
* Time from first to second purchase

### Target direction

The immediate objective should be to **increase the repeat-customer rate materially above the current 3.00% baseline**, rather than focusing only on acquiring additional first-time customers.

# 3\. Priority 2 — Protect High-Value Customers

### Why this matters

The highest-value customer generated approximately **R$13,440**.

This demonstrates that customer value is highly uneven. Losing a small number of highly valuable customers can have a disproportionate commercial impact.

### Recommended actions

#### 3.1 Create a high-value customer segment

Identify customers based on cumulative spending and purchase frequency.

For example:

* Top-value customers
* High-frequency customers
* High-spend but low-frequency customers
* Recently inactive high-value customers

### 3.2 Create retention safeguards

For high-value customers:

* Prioritize service recovery when an order is delayed.
* Monitor negative reviews or complaints.
* Provide targeted offers rather than generic promotions.
* Encourage repeat purchases through relevant recommendations.
* Monitor their delivery and seller experience.

### 3.3 Monitor customer concentration

Management should track how much revenue comes from the top customer segments.

This creates an early-warning system for customer concentration risk.

### KPIs

* Revenue from top 1%, 5% and 10% of customers
* Retention rate of high-value customers
* Revenue at risk from inactive high-value customers
* Average spend by customer segment
* Repeat rate by customer segment

# 4\. Priority 3 — Maintain and Optimize Payment Flexibility

### Why this matters

Installment customers:

* **48,208 records**
* **R$204.40 average value**

Non-installment customers:

* **45,149 records**
* **R$123.34 average value**

Installment customers therefore had an average value approximately **65.7% higher**.

The payment-method analysis also showed:

|Payment Method|Records|Average Value|
|-|-:|-:|
|Credit Card|72,027|R$168.01|
|Boleto|18,717|R$147.99|
|Debit Card|1,470|R$141.78|
|Voucher|3,591|R$95.52|

Credit card had the highest average value, while voucher had the lowest.

### Important qualification

These results show **association, not causation**. The analysis does not prove that installments cause customers to spend more. Higher-value customers may simply be more likely to choose installments.

### Recommended actions

#### 4.1 Protect credit-card checkout performance

Because credit-card transactions have the highest average value:

* Monitor payment success rates.
* Reduce payment failures.
* Monitor checkout abandonment.
* Ensure installment options are clearly displayed.
* Monitor payment processing performance during peak periods.

#### 4.2 Optimize installment options

Test:

* Number of available installments
* Minimum order value for installments
* Promotional installment offers
* Visibility of installment information
* Installment availability by category

The objective should be to determine whether payment flexibility increases conversion or order value after controlling for customer and product characteristics.

#### 4.3 Investigate voucher behavior

Voucher transactions have an average value of **R$95.52**, substantially below credit-card transactions at **R$168.01**.

This does not mean vouchers should be reduced automatically.

Instead, investigate:

* Which customers use vouchers?
* Which categories are purchased with vouchers?
* Are voucher customers new or repeat buyers?
* Are voucher orders associated with promotions?
* Does voucher usage increase customer acquisition or retention?

### KPIs

* Payment success rate
* Checkout conversion rate
* Average order value by payment method
* Installment adoption rate
* Repeat-purchase rate by payment method
* Payment failure rate
* Cart abandonment rate

### Recommended actions

#### 5.1.1 Regional logistics diagnosis

For high-risk states, investigate:

* Carrier performance
* Average transportation distance
* Seller location
* Fulfillment location
* Processing time
* Delivery route
* Order volume
* Regional demand spikes

The objective is to determine whether the problem is caused by sellers, carriers, geography or capacity constraints.

Create carrier-level KPIs:

* On-time delivery rate
* Average delivery days
* Late-delivery rate
* Delivery variance
* Performance by state
* Performance during peak periods

Underperforming carriers should be investigated or replaced where appropriate.

# 6\. Priority 5 — Protect Customer Satisfaction Through Delivery

The satisfaction analysis creates an important connection between delivery performance and customer experience.

The analysis compares reviews across:

* delivered early,
* delivered on time,
* delivered late.

The supplied results showed a substantial difference between early and late delivery satisfaction, with approximately:

* **Early delivery: 4.29 average review**
* **Late delivery: 2.27 average review**

This is a major management signal.

### Business implication

Delivery should not be treated only as a logistics KPI.

It should be treated as a **customer-experience and retention KPI**.

### Recommended actions

#### 6.1 Prioritize at-risk orders

When an order becomes likely to miss its estimated delivery date:

* identify it before the deadline,
* notify the customer,
* provide an updated delivery estimate,
* offer appropriate service recovery,
* monitor the resulting review.

#### 6.2 Introduce delivery-risk monitoring

Build a dashboard showing:

* orders approaching estimated delivery dates,
* orders already late,
* late orders by state,
* late orders by seller,
* late orders by carrier,
* customer reviews associated with late orders.

### KPIs

* On-time delivery rate
* Late-delivery rate
* Average review score by delivery status
* Review score for late orders
* Customer retention after late delivery
* Customer-service contacts caused by delivery

# 7\. Priority 6 — Prepare for Revenue Peaks

Delivered-order revenue reached:

* **R$987,765.37 in November 2017**
* **R$977,544.69 in May 2018**
* **R$973,534.09 in April 2018**

November 2017 also recorded approximately **+52.37% month-over-month growth**, followed by a **-26.50% decline in December**.

This indicates meaningful demand volatility.

### Recommended actions

Before expected peak periods:

* Increase seller capacity planning.
* Identify high-demand product categories.
* Check inventory availability.
* Increase logistics capacity.
* Prepare customer-support resources.
* Monitor payment-system capacity.
* Monitor carrier capacity.
* Prepare contingency plans for late deliveries.

### Peak-period dashboard

Management should monitor daily:

* Orders
* Revenue
* Average order value
* Payment success rate
* Order-processing time
* Late-delivery risk
* Customer-service contacts
* Review scores

### KPI

The key objective is:

> \*\*Grow peak-period revenue without allowing delivery and customer satisfaction to deteriorate.\*\*

# 8\. Priority 7 — Optimize the Product and Category Mix

The analysis identified:

* Highest-revenue product: approximately **R$63,560**
* Highest-volume product: **520 units / R$37,104.30**

These are not necessarily the same product, demonstrating why volume alone is insufficient.

### Recommended actions

Evaluate categories using four dimensions:

1. Sales volume
2. Total revenue
3. Revenue per item
4. Customer satisfaction

### Create a category portfolio

#### High demand + high value

Invest in:

* inventory availability,
* seller capacity,
* promotions,
* visibility.

#### High demand + low value

Investigate:

* pricing,
* bundles,
* cross-selling,
* upselling.

#### Low demand + high value

Investigate:

* discoverability,
* marketing,
* product placement,
* target customer segments.

#### Low demand + low value

Consider:

* reducing catalog emphasis,
* improving product selection,
* removing underperforming listings where appropriate.

### Satisfaction integration

Categories with low reviews should be investigated together with:

* seller performance,
* delivery performance,
* product quality,
* customer expectations,
* order characteristics.

# 9\. Priority 8 — Strengthen High-Performing Sellers While Reducing Concentration Risk

The seller analysis identifies sellers whose delivered-order revenue is above the marketplace-wide seller average.

Top sellers are commercially important, but excessive dependence on a small group creates platform risk.

### Recommended actions

#### 9.1 Protect high-performing sellers

Provide:

* performance insights,
* logistics support,
* visibility opportunities,
* seller education,
* faster issue resolution.

#### 9.2 Develop emerging sellers

Identify sellers that are:

* growing quickly,
* receiving strong reviews,
* generating high revenue per order,
* selling products in high-demand categories.

Help these sellers scale before the marketplace becomes overly dependent on a small number of established sellers.

#### 9.3 Monitor seller concentration

Track the percentage of marketplace revenue generated by:

* top 1 seller,
* top 5 sellers,
* top 10 sellers,
* top 10% of sellers.

### KPIs

* Seller revenue
* Revenue per order
* Seller growth rate
* Seller retention
* Seller concentration
* Seller review score
* Seller late-delivery rate

# 10\. Priority 9 — Build a Cross-Functional Retention Strategy

The analysis suggests that customer retention is not controlled by one department.

A customer is more likely to return when several parts of the marketplace work together:

**Product → Seller → Payment → Delivery → Satisfaction → Repeat Purchase**

### Recommended approach

Create a retention dashboard that connects:

* first order,
* order value,
* product category,
* seller,
* payment method,
* delivery status,
* review score,
* second purchase.

This allows management to identify combinations associated with successful retention.

### Example question

Instead of asking:

> "How many customers returned?"

Ask:

> "What product, seller, payment and delivery characteristics are most common among customers who returned?"

That question produces much more actionable business intelligence.

# 14\. Final Business Recommendation

The most important strategic recommendation is:

> \*\*Do not treat revenue growth as the final objective. Convert revenue growth into repeat customer value while protecting delivery reliability, seller quality, payment flexibility and customer satisfaction.\*\*

The data supports a clear sequence:

**1. Acquire customers → 2. Deliver a strong first experience → 3. Convert them to a second purchase → 4. Increase customer lifetime value → 5. Protect the operational system that enables retention.**

The strongest immediate opportunity is the gap between the **3.00% repeat-customer rate** and the much higher value generated by repeat customers.

At the same time, the large difference between early and late-delivery review scores means that retention cannot be solved through marketing alone. Operational execution is part of the customer-retention strategy.

Payment flexibility should support this strategy, especially because installment customers and credit-card transactions show higher average values, while the relationship should be validated with deeper analysis before making causal claims.

Overall, Olist should move toward a **customer-value-driven marketplace strategy**, where growth is measured not only by order volume and revenue, but also by:

**customer retention + customer lifetime value + delivery reliability + seller quality + customer satisfaction.**

\---

## Methodology Notes

* Revenue recommendations are based on **delivered orders only**.
* The repeat-customer definition is a `customer\_unique\_id` associated with more than one delivered order.
* Late delivery means the actual customer delivery date is later than the estimated delivery date.
* Payment-method counts should be interpreted according to the underlying query's unit of analysis and are not automatically unique customers.
* Payment relationships are associations and should not be interpreted as causal without additional analysis.
